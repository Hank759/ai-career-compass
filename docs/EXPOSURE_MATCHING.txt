Exposure matching policy - version 4.7

Current BLS occupations are retained even when exposure data is missing.
Default lists contain all 830 occupations. Optional exposure-only filters on
Home and data pages keep occupations with at least one available metric.

Exact source-code records take precedence per metric. The reviewed whitelist
contains eight one-to-one computer occupation pairs from the official 2018 SOC
Manual, checked in Appendices A and B (PDF pages 230 and 252):
2018 SOC -> 2010 SOC
15-1211 -> 15-1121
15-1212 -> 15-1122
15-1221 -> 15-1111
15-1231 -> 15-1152
15-1232 -> 15-1151
15-1241 -> 15-1143
15-1244 -> 15-1142
15-1251 -> 15-1131
Source: https://www.bls.gov/soc/2018/soc_2018_manual.pdf

Scores are unchanged historical references, not newly measured current-category
estimates. Original source SOC, matching method, file and crosswalk URL are
retained in source details and target report exports. The mapping does not
establish unchanged tasks or current AI capabilities. Definitions may have
editorial changes. No title-only guesses, splitting, weighted averaging or
multi-predecessor merging are performed. Other unmatched codes remain missing.

Coverage: 673 exact-code occupations plus eight reviewed historical references
equals 681 of 830 occupations with at least one metric; 149 remain unmatched.
Missing or partially missing metrics are labelled with an explicit statement
that no available data match exists at present. Missing data is never zero.

Dashboard, recommendations, comparisons, reports and Home coverage share the
same resolver. Exposure remains a comparison reference, not a ranking term.
Raw data and the database snapshot are unchanged. Resolved results are cached
read-only by database path, modification timestamp and size; callers get copies.

Verification: 106 regression tests passed, including exact source-value retention,
exact-record precedence, split/merge exclusions, missing-data wording, filtering
and unchanged recommendation score formulas. The isolated live runtime also
checked all eleven pages and the comparison-to-report workflow.
